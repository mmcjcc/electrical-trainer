# NEC (NFPA 70) section and table crosswalk, 2020 → 2026, with 2023

Prepared 2026-10-16 for the electrician exam-prep app. The exam uses the 2020 NEC now and is expected to move straight to the 2026 NEC, so the **2026 column comes right after 2020**. The 2023 column is kept wherever it was found.

The tables give only section and table numbers, short titles, and the value of each rule. They do not reproduce code text.

## How to read this

- **Columns:** concept | value/rule | NEC 2020 | **NEC 2026** | NEC 2023 | source URL(s) | confidence | notes. Each row has at least one source URL that was actually downloaded or read.
- **Confidence**
  - **High:** two or more sources agree, at least one of them NFPA's own committee record, or a source quoting the published code text.
  - **Med-high / Medium:** one strong source, or a secondary source only.
  - **Low:** unresolved.
- **"(draft)"** in the 2026 column means the 2026 number or value is confirmed only by NFPA's 2026-cycle working documents: First Draft (FD) / Second Draft (SD) panel output and ballots. Those passed panel ballot, but floor motions could still change the published book; one did, for 120.13. **Verify these against the published 2026 code before using them on an exam.**
- **"(same suffix)"** in a load-calculation row means no source discusses the section. It is assumed to have become 120.xx with the same suffix as 2023 and no change.
- **Rows confirmed in only one edition** say so in the notes, or show "—" for an edition where the rule doesn't exist.
- **Main source families**
  - NFPA committee documents at `https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/70/`:
    - The "A2022" cycle produced the 2023 NEC. Its base text is the **2020** NEC.
    - The "A2025" cycle produced the 2026 NEC. Its base text is the **2023** NEC.
    - Section numbers in their headings are base-edition numbers.
  - The NFPA 2026 NEC Handbook errata replacement page.
  - ElectricalLicenseRenewal.com (ELR) code-change pages, which quote the old and new code language for each edition.
  - Mike Holt (2020/2023 free PDFs; the 2026 Calculations guide table of contents, which cites 2026 section numbers).
  - EC&M (including articles by NFPA staff), IAEI, CSE, EFCOG (DOE contractors' 2020-vs-2023 comparison), ICC, Kopperfield.
- Downloaded working files are under `scratchpad\research\nec-crosswalk-dl`, `nec-dl-conductors`, and `nec-dl-motors`. They were treated as data only.

## A. Load calculations: 2020 Article 220 → 2026 Article 120 (2023 Article 220)

**What moved.** In 2026, all of Article 220 was moved to a new **Article 120 in Chapter 1**. Most section numbers keep their suffix: 220.xx in 2023 becomes 120.xx. Sources: [ELR 2026 Art. 120](https://www.electricallicenserenewal.com/Electrical-Continuing-Education-Courses/NEC-Content.php?sectionID=1841), [ELR 2026 global changes](https://www.electricallicenserenewal.com/Electrical-Continuing-Education-Courses/NEC-Content.php?sectionID=1808), [EC&M, R. Jackson, Jul 2026](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/code-basics/article/55387143/global-code-wide-changes-and-overview).

The suffixes a 2020 user knows are not the 2026 suffixes. The 2023 edition had already rearranged Part III. NFPA First Revision FR-9188 describes this as a reorganization with no technical change:
- 220.11 → 220.5(C)
- 220.12 → 220.42
- 220.42 → 220.45
- 220.43 → 220.46
- 220.44 → 220.47
- 220.14(J) → 220.41
- 220.14(M) → 220.44
- 220.18 → 220.11

In 2026 the 2023 suffix carries over. So **2020 220.12 becomes 2026 120.42** and **2020 Table 220.42 becomes 2026 Table 120.45**.

On top of the moves, 2026 makes real technical changes:
- Dwelling unit load: 2 VA/ft² instead of 3 VA/ft²
- Optional-method first block: 8 kVA instead of 10 kVA
- New dryer demand table
- New single table for existing dwellings
- Load calculations no longer require 125% for continuous loads

**How to read the 2026 column.** Most 2026 findings are confirmed by sources that quote the published 2026 text: ElectricalLicenseRenewal (ELR) 2026 change pages, EC&M articles by NFPA staff, and 2026 practitioner discussion. Findings marked **"(draft)"** come only from NFPA's 2026-cycle working drafts (First Draft / Second Draft panel output). Check those against the published 2026 code. One example of why: a Second Draft change to 120.13 (4 VA/ft²) was reversed on the floor back to 3 VA/ft² ([ELR 120.13](https://www.electricallicenserenewal.com/Electrical-Continuing-Education-Courses/NEC-Content.php?sectionID=1844)). Findings marked **"(same suffix)"** were not found in any change source. They are assumed to have been renumbered 220.xx → 120.xx with no change.

## Biggest renumberings an exam candidate will trip over (2020 → 2026)

### Load calculations
1. **Article 220 is now Article 120 (Chapter 1).** Every load-calculation citation changes. Most 2026 numbers are the 2023 number with "120" in front, and the 2023 numbers had already moved away from 2020:
   - Non-dwelling lighting unit loads: 2020 **Table 220.12** → 2026 **Table 120.42(A)**
   - Lighting demand factors: 2020 **Table 220.42** → 2026 **Table 120.45**
   - Show-window and track lighting: 2020 **220.43** → 2026 **120.46**
   - Non-dwelling receptacle demand: 2020 **220.44 / Table 220.44** → 2026 **120.47 / Table 120.47**
   - Dwelling unit load: 2020 **220.14(J)** → 2026 **120.41**
   - Floor area: 2020 **220.11** → 2026 **120.5(C)**
   - Hotel/motel guest rooms: 2020 **220.14(M)** → 2026 **120.44**
   - Office receptacles: 2020 **220.14(K)** → 2026 **120.14(J)**
   - Other outlets: 2020 **220.14(L)** → 2026 **120.14(K)**
   - Motor/combination loads at 125%: 2020 **220.18(A)** → 2026 **120.11(A)**
   - Noncoincident loads: 2020 **220.60** → 2026 **120.6**
   - Multifamily table: 2020 **Table 220.84** → 2023 Table 220.84(B) → 2026 **Table 120.84(C)** (draft)
2. **2026 load-calculation values a 2020 candidate would get wrong:**
   - Dwelling unit load is **2 VA/ft²** (120.41). 3 VA/ft² stays only for counting branch circuits (new **120.13**).
   - Optional one-dwelling method uses the **first 8 kVA** at 100% (was 10 kVA) (120.82).
   - Existing dwellings use one **Table 120.83**: 100% / 40% / new EVSE 80% / new resistance heat 80% / other new loads 50%.
   - Dryers, **Table 120.54**: 1–2 at 100%, 3–5 at 80%.
   - The "125% included" note under the lighting table is **deleted**, and **120.5(E)** says load calculations don't require 125% for continuous loads.
   - Floor area includes attached garages (from 2023) but not **detached garages** (2026).
   - School/university and sports arena unit loads are **1.5 VA/ft²** (from 2023; 2020 was 3).
3. **Wrong in many study sources:**
   - The lighting demand table has **no hospital row** (removed in 2020).
   - Hotel/motel factors are **60/50/35%** (not 50/40/30).
   - The Table 220.55 → 120.55 branch-circuit notes have been **Notes 4, 5, 6** since 2023 (Note 4 alone in 2020).

### Conductors, overcurrent protection, fill
4. **Feeders:** 2020 **215.2(A)(1)** (125% continuous) → 2026 **215.4(A)(1)**. Feeder OCPD 2020 **215.3** → 2026 **215.5**. Feeder voltage-drop note: 2020 215.2(A)(1) Informational Note No. 2 → 2026 215.4(A)(2) Informational Note No. 2 (inferred).
5. **Branch circuits:** 2020 **210.19(A)(1)(a)** → 2023/2026 **210.19(A)(1)**. The voltage-drop note moves from 2020 **210.19(A) Informational Note No. 3** to one unnumbered 210.19 Informational Note. The often-quoted "210.19(A)(1) Informational Note No. 4" is the 2017 number.
6. **Ambient correction tables:** 2020 **Table 310.15(B)(1)** (30 °C) and **Table 310.15(B)(2)** (40 °C) → 2023/2026 **Table 310.15(B)(1)(1)** and **(B)(1)(2)**. "310.15(B)(2)" now means the rooftop rule, and its distance changed from 7/8 in. to **3/4 in.** in 2023.
7. **Neutral rule 310.15(E) flips in 2026.** (E)(1) becomes "Current-Carrying" (it used to be the "not counted" rule) and (E)(2) becomes "Non-Current-Carrying".
8. **Dwelling 83% table:** 2020 **Table 310.12** → 2023/2026 **Table 310.12(A)**. In 2026 it applies only to conductors rated 75 °C or higher.
9. **240.4(D) small-conductor items shift by one in 2023**, after the new 14 AWG copper-clad aluminum item (D)(3). 12 AWG Cu (20 A) is (D)(5) in 2020 but (D)(6) in 2023/2026; 10 AWG Cu (30 A) is (D)(7) → (D)(8). **Table 240.6(A)** gains 10 A (2023).
10. **Box-fill volume table:** 2020 **Table 314.16(B)** → 2023 **Table 314.16(B)(1)**. The 2026 label is unresolved. New 314.16(B)(6) terminal blocks arrived in 2023 without renumbering (B)(1)–(5).
11. **Minimum conductor size** moves from 310.3(A) to **310.5(A)** in 2026, which also adds 16 AWG Cu and 14 AWG CCA rows to Table 310.16.

### Motors, transformers, grounding, protection devices
12. **Motor short-circuit/ground-fault table:** 2020 **Table 430.52** → 2023/2026 **Table 430.52(C)(1)**.
13. **Transformer overcurrent tables:** 2020/2023 **Table 450.3(B)** (and 450.3(A)) → 2026 **Table 450.5(B)** (and 450.5(A)). Article 450 shifts by 2 in 2026. In 2020/2023, **450.5** is *grounding autotransformers*, so a 2020 student who looks up "450.5" will find the wrong topic.
