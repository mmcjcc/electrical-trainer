# Maryland Master Electrician License Exam: research report (status as of 2026-10-16)

All sources were accessed on **2026-10-16** unless noted. "Primary" means the Maryland Department of Labor (MD Labor), the State Board of Electricians, the exam vendor (PSI), the Maryland General Assembly statute site, COMAR (regs.maryland.gov), or the Maryland Register. Contents of downloaded files are treated as data.

**A correction to the brief:** the electrician statute is the **Business Occupations and Professions Article (BOP), Title 6**, the "Maryland Electricians Act" (BOP §6-701). It is not the Business Regulation Article. The Business Regulation Article is cited only for fee mechanics (BR §§2-106.9 and 2-106.10, cited in BOP §6-207 and COMAR 09.09.01.01).

**Naming:** the statute calls the regulator the **State Board of Electricians** (BOP §6-101(l), §6-201). The PSI bulletin and some MD Labor pages still say "State Board of Master Electricians".

---

## Key findings

- **Vendor:** PSI Services LLC. The exam is computer-based and open book.
- **Format:** **90 questions worth 100 points**, **240 minutes**, **70% to pass**. Electrical Theory is ten 2-point items.
- **Weighting:** **Calculations is 30 of the 100 points**, the largest single area.
- **Code edition:** the only reference allowed is the **2020 NEC (NFPA 70)**. As of Nov 2025, PSI told the Board it was still testing on the 2020 code. No date to switch the exam to a newer NEC had been announced as of 2026-10-16.
- **Next code cycle:** the Board seems to be skipping the 2023 NEC for testing and going to the **2026 NEC**.
  - Board staff asked PSI about updated 2026 exam materials.
  - MUELEC was expected to finish draft 2026-NEC questions by the end of August 2026.
  - A proposed rule (COMAR 09.09.03.04) would start Board *enforcement* of the 2026 NEC on Aug 31, 2026. No final action had been published by the Oct 16, 2026 Register.
- **Statewide adoption is mixed:**
  - DLI's construction-code rules still reference the **2020 NEC**. DLI's proposal to move to the 2023 NEC was **withdrawn** in the Oct 16, 2026 Maryland Register.
  - Local codes differ. Baltimore City uses the 2020 NEC. Montgomery County still lists the 2017 NEC.
- **Pending rule:** a regulation proposed on Oct 16, 2026 would require **3 years as a licensed journeyperson** before master eligibility. Comments close Nov 16, 2026, and final action is set for Nov 24, 2026.
- **Pass rates are low.** Monthly master pass rates are typically about 24-32%, with a range of 16% to 43% in late 2024 to mid 2026. About 28% of all exams since inception have been passed.

## 2. Exam format

All from the PSI bulletin (https://test-takers.psiexams.com/api/content/bulletin/8041, "Updated 9/1/2024", current PSI copy) unless noted.

| Item | Master Electrician |
|---|---|
| Scored questions | **90 (100 points)**. The bulletin's outline says Electrical Theory is "Ten 2-point items" worth 20.<br>The area numbers add to 100, so the other 80 points are presumably 80 one-point items: 10 + 80 = 90 questions. This split is my arithmetic. The bulletin does not state the point value of non-theory items. |
| Unscored items | "a small number of five to ten 'experimental' questions" may be added. They are not scored, and their time does not count against exam time. |
| Time limit | **240 minutes**, about 2.7 minutes per scored item. A tutorial of up to 15 minutes before the exam does not count. |
| Passing score | **70%**. The statute also fixes 70% (BOP §6-306(f)). |
| Open or closed book | **OPEN BOOK.** "THIS IS THE ONLY REFERENCE ALLOWED IN THE TESTING ROOM": NFPA 70, 2020. See section 5. |
| Question type | Multiple choice. All 8 sample questions (A-H) have **4 options**, and some use "None of the above" (sample H's correct answer is "None of the above"). The bulletin does not explicitly state "4-option". |
| Delivery | **Computer-based** ("The examination will be administered via computer. You will be using a mouse and computer keyboard"). One question appears on screen at a time, and the time remaining is shown. Candidates can go back and change answers while time remains. A comments key is available. |
| Separate portions | **None.** It is one exam with one overall score.<br>Code articles, theory and calculations are all content areas within the single exam.<br>There is **no separate Maryland law or business portion** and no law topic in the outline, even though the statute says questions test "codes, laws, or principles" (BOP §6-306(d)(2)).<br>Results are pass/fail on screen. Failing candidates also get section scores by outline area and 30 minutes to review missed items. |
| Fee and retake | $65, valid one year, non-refundable and non-transferable. Cancel or reschedule at least 2 days ahead, or the fee is forfeited.<br>Retake wait: **30 days** (bulletin; COMAR 09.09.01.04).<br>MD Labor FAQ #5 says later retests "cannot be made until 60 days have passed". |
| Test centers (bulletin) | Baltimore (6115 Falls Rd), College Park, Crofton, Hagerstown, Laurel (Mid-Atlantic Testing Co.), Salisbury (Brainseed). Any PSI center nationally is also allowed.<br>The MD Labor exam page lists "Baltimore, College Park, Hagerstown, Lanham, and Salisbury". The two lists differ. |

**Journeyperson for comparison:** 70 questions, 49 (70%) to pass, 210 minutes, same reference and rules.

### Announced switch date: none found

**No exam switch date had been announced as of 2026-10-16.** The trail in Board records:

- **Nov 2025:** the Board expected the 2023 NEC "would be used for both testing and enforcement when it was adopted." PSI had previously let candidates choose old or new versions "for a few months" after a code change. (elec-nov2025.docx)
- **Jan 27, 2026:** the Board formed an **"NEC 2026 Committee"**.
  - Counsel advised that BOP §6-205(a)(3) requires the Board to *enforce* the most recent NEC within 18 months of issuance. She said the Board "may also begin testing on the updated code at an appropriate time".
  - The Board favoured starting enforcement of each new edition in August of the year after publication.
  - Source: https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-jan2026.shtml
- **Feb-Jul 2026:** MUELEC is drafting "proposed examination questions for the 2026 National Electrical Code examination".
  - The target slipped from June 30, 2026 (May and June minutes) to "the end of August 2026" (July minutes).
  - Staff "contacted PSI regarding the availability of updated 2026 examination materials".
  - Sources: https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-feb2026.shtml , ...elec-may2026.shtml , ...elec-june2026.shtml , https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-jul2026.shtml
- **Precedent for the last transition:**
  - The exam moved from the 2017 NEC to the 2020 NEC. PSI planned to start the 2020-based exam on **July 1, 2023**.
  - The Board voted to negotiate a window in which candidates could choose the 2017 or 2020 version. Counsel noted other mechanical boards use 3-to-6-month choice windows.
  - The minutes also say enforcement is "not necessarily tied to the examination standard".
  - Source: Jan 24, 2023 minutes, https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-jan2023.pdf
- **Recent agendas:** the Aug 25, 2026 and Oct 2, 2026 agendas still list "2026 NEC Committee" and "COMAR 09.09.03.04 - Update on regulatory action on enforcement of NEC". Minutes for those meetings were not yet posted. Sources: https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-agendaaug2026.shtml , https://labor.maryland.gov/license/min/elec-agendasep2026.shtml

For the app, expect the exam to jump from 2020 to the 2026 NEC rather than to 2023, probably with a transition window. This is inferred from the Board records above. It has not been announced.

## 5. Allowed references and calculator rules

From the PSI bulletin, pp.3-4 and pp.7-8.

**Allowed reference:**
- "National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, **2020**, National Fire Protection Association..."
- "THIS REFERENCE WILL NOT BE PROVIDED AT THE TEST CENTER. **THIS IS THE ONLY REFERENCE ALLOWED IN THE TESTING ROOM.**"

**Open-book rules:**
- "Candidates may bring in reference books. However, **no study guides** are allowed."
- "Reference books may be **indexed**, and may contain **highlighted or underlined** text."
- "All materials must be **unmarked (not written in)** and may **not contain additional papers (loose or attached)**."

**Covers, handbooks and other books:**
- **Softcover vs hardcover:** not addressed in the bulletin.
- **Ugly's, Maryland law books, and the NFPA 70 Handbook with commentary:** not on the reference list. Since the NEC is "the only reference allowed", treat them as not allowed. The bulletin does not address the Handbook by name.

**Tabs:**
- Acceptable: "Avery Swift Tabs Self-Adhesive Permanent Plastic Tabs; Redi-Tag Self-Stick Permanent Adhesive Index Tabs."
- Unacceptable: "Post-It Index Flags; Post-It Flags."
- "If the PSI proctor is able to remove the tabs without ripping the page, you will need to remove the tabs."
- "Do not use the tabs that have paper inserts. The paper inserts will be removed."

**Calculator:**
- "**You will have access to an online calculator.**"
- Personal belongings go to secure storage, including "electronic devices of any type", "reference or reading material", "pens, pencils".
- The bulletin does not list a personal calculator as an approved item. Plan for the on-screen calculator only.
- Scratch paper is not addressed.

**Other conflict rules stated in the bulletin:**
- A legal requirement (code, law or regulation) overrides other references.
- A State-specific code or law overrides a national one.
- The test reference list overrides other sources.

**PSI bulletin tips (primary):**
- Study from the content outline and "learn the major points" of each topic.
- Underline or highlight key ideas, which is allowed in the NEC.
- Review the Code until each idea is understood.
- Check topics off and test when all are checked.
- Study "frequently, for about 45 to 60 minutes" at a time.
- A failing score report breaks scores out by outline section.
- Failing candidates get 30 minutes to review missed items.

**Weighting implications (derived from the outline):**
- Calculations alone is 30% of points.
- Theory, which includes math, conversions and Ohm's law, is another 20%.
- So at least half of the master exam is computational or theory. A candidate can get every NEC-lookup item right (50 points) and still fail without the calculations.
- The 2-point theory items carry double weight.
- Special Communications (2 points) and Special Equipment (4 points) are small.

**No primary source gives per-section failure statistics.** The Board receives only overall pass and fail counts. A 2026 request for demographic and pass-rate analysis was referred to PSI (Feb-Apr 2026 minutes).
