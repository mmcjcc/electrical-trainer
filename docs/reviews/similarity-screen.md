# Similarity screen and rewriting pass of the question bank, 2026-10-22

Every question in `src/content/bank/` is meant to be our own work, never a copy of a publisher’s or vendor’s item
(CLAUDE.md, Copyright). 318 of the 365 items were first drafted by Antigravity (Gemini) from memory and then checked
and partly rewritten by Claude (`docs/reviews/bank-*.md`). A model can echo text it saw in training, and NEC practice
questions are all over the web, so this pass checked every item against the open web and gave a fresh rewrite to the
items that were still close to their AI drafts. Screen and rewrites by Claude; the per-item log is at the end.

## Summary

- **365 of 365 items** were screened with an exact-phrase web search (391 searches in all).
- **The screen found no item that reproduces a question from a publisher, vendor or practice bank.** For 25 items the search surfaced a quiz or
  prep page on the same rule; each was opened and compared. **7** shared a scenario, an option set or close wording with one and
  were rewritten (below); the rest ask a different question or use different options.
- **184 items were rewritten** in new words: 137 of the 137 that were still close to their AI draft, plus
  47 others (42 whose stem or key read like the code’s own sentence, 5 flagged by the screen). Each keeps its id,
  topic, kind, difficulty, editions, reference, answer position and numbers, except where a flagged item needed new
  distractors or a new worked example (listed below).
- **PSI bulletin samples:** the eight sample questions (A-H) in PSI’s candidate bulletin were compared with the whole bank
  by hand. Only `wiring-methods-062` tested the same fact as a sample (D, fill in auxiliary gutters); its words and options
  were already different, and it was recast as a wireway job anyway.

## How the screen was done

1. **Exact-phrase search.** For each stem, the most distinctive run of 8-12 consecutive words (scenario details and the
   question’s own phrasing, not generic openers) was searched in double quotes, one standard web search per item.
2. **Classes.** `none`: no page contains the fragment. `nec`: the nearest pages quote or explain the code rule (or, for
   Maryland law items, the statute). `generic`: the words match unrelated pages. `QUESTION`: a quiz, practice test,
   flashcard deck or prep page asks about the same thing. Every `QUESTION` page was opened and compared for the same
   scenario (numbers and setting) and the same options.
3. **Flag rule.** A `QUESTION` hit with the same scenario or the same option set is flagged and rewritten: new wording,
   and new distractors or a new scenario where the rule allows, with the same rule, answer and reference.
4. **Code wording.** Where the screen showed a stem or key reading like the code’s sentence, the item was reworded in plain
   words, in the voice the app uses everywhere else. Statute wording in the Maryland law items was left alone (public law).
   Six wiring-protection items the last screen called minor echoes (057, 062, 066, 089, 092, 098) restate the rule in
   ordinary words already and were left as they are.
5. **Wording screened.** Most items were searched as they read before this pass. Items rewritten before their turn came
   were searched in their new wording, and flagged items were searched again after the rewrite. 275 items were
   searched in their final wording; the others were searched before their rewrite (column "wording" in the log).

## How "close to the draft" was measured

The raw Antigravity drafts (`agy-briefs/etrainer-bank/out/*.questions.json`, kept outside the repo) were compared with
the live items by word-level longest-common-subsequence ratio (0 = nothing in common, 1 = identical), separately for the
stem, the four options (each option against its closest draft option) and the explanation. An item was selected for a
rewrite when its weighted score (stem 0.45, options 0.30, explanation 0.25; when the options are bare numbers, stem 0.6 and
explanation 0.4) was 0.6 or more, or its stem alone was 0.7 or more: 137 items. The target after rewriting was a stem and
an explanation below 0.5 and word options below 0.6. Options that are numbers, sizes, ratings or fixed code labels keep
their values, so they still score high.

Across the 177 rewritten items that have a draft: stem median 0.68 → 0.29 (highest after 0.48),
explanation median 0.47 → 0.31 (highest after 0.49).

## Limits

- An exact-phrase search finds only text that a web search engine has indexed. Paid question banks, printed prep books
  and apps are not covered, and a page that copies a scenario in other words can slip past a phrase search; the quiz and
  prep pages that did surface were compared by scenario and options, not only by words.
- No n-gram comparison against printed prep books and no embedding (near-paraphrase) check were run in this pass.
- The rewrites were made by Claude, not by a person. They move the items away from the AI drafts; they do not make the
  bank human-written.
- Search results are as of 2026-10-22.
